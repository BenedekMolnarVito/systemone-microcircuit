## 1. Canonical cortical microcircuit

- Douglas RJ, Martin KAC (1991). "A functional microcircuit for cat visual cortex." J Physiol 440:735-769. DOI 10.1113/jphysiol.1991.sp018733. https://doi.org/10.1113/jphysiol.1991.sp018733. Crossref Y.
  - Claim: canonical intracortical circuit; thalamic input → L4 → L2/3 → L5/6; L4 spiny stellates dominant thalamic recipients.
- Douglas RJ, Martin KAC (2004). "Neuronal circuits of the neocortex." Annu Rev Neurosci 27:419-451. DOI 10.1146/annurev.neuro.27.070203.144152. Crossref Y (title caps in crossref).
  - Claim: review of same canonical circuit; feedforward L4→L2/3→L5 and recurrent excitation/inhibition.
- Potjans TC, Diesmann M (2014). "The cell-type specific cortical microcircuit: relating structure and activity in a full-scale spiking network model." Cereb Cortex 24:785-806. DOI 10.1093/cercor/bhs358. PMID 23203991, PMCID PMC3920768. Crossref Y (crossref epub year 2012, print vol 24 = 2014). https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3920768/

## 2. LIF params + escape noise

- Brunel 2000 (above) and Potjans Table 5 (above): tau_m 10 ms; t_ref 2 ms; theta -50 mV; V_reset -65 mV; C_m 250 pF; tau_syn 0.5 ms; w_exc 87.8 ± 8.8 pA; g=-4; d_e 1.5 ± 0.75 ms; d_i 0.8 ± 0.4 ms; PSP ≈0.15 mV mean (Methods).
- Gerstner W (2000). "Population dynamics of spiking neurons: fast transients, asynchronous states, and locking." Neural Comput 12:43-89. DOI 10.1162/089976600300015899. Crossref Y.
  - Claim: escape-noise (stochastic threshold) population dynamics; firing intensity ρ(u) depends on distance to threshold. Sigmoid form NOT confirmed in abstract. Treat "p=sigmoid((V-θ)/Δ)" as a modeling choice motivated by escape rate ρ=ρ0 exp((V-θ)/Δ) (Gerstner & Kistler 2002, Ch. 5 escape noise). Crossref Y for paper; sigmoid-specific claim = N.
- Gerstner & Kistler 2002 (above) — escape-noise chapter; Δ ≈ 2-5 mV typical (N, from memory, verify in book ch. 5).
- Jolivet R et al. (2006) generalized IF fits: N (not searched; add if needed).

## 3. Interneuron motifs

- Pfeffer CK, Xue M, He M, Huang ZJ, Scanziani M (2013). "Inhibition of inhibition in visual cortex: the logic of connections between molecularly distinct interneurons." Nat Neurosci 16:1068-1076. DOI 10.1038/nn.3446. Crossref Y.
  - Claim: SST cells inhibit PV cells (and PV perisomatic inhibition of pyramids); VIP→SST disinhibition motif.
- Pi HJ, Hangya B, Kvitsiani D, Sanders JI, Huang ZJ, Kepecs A (2013). "Cortical interneurons that specialize in disinhibitory control." Nature 503:521-524. DOI 10.1038/nature12676. Crossref Y.
  - Claim: VIP cells inhibit SST (and other interneurons) → disinhibit pyramids; gating role in behavior.
- Tremblay R, Lee S, Rudy B (2016). "GABAergic interneurons in the neocortex: from cellular properties to circuits." Neuron 91:260-292. DOI 10.1016/j.neuron.2016.06.033. Crossref Y.
  - Claim: PV = perisomatic/fast-spiking; SST = dendritic-targeting, facilitating synapses; VIP = disinhibitory.

## 4. Retina/LGN, orientation

- Kuffler SW (1953). "Discharge patterns and functional organization of mammalian retina." J Neurophysiol 16:37-68. DOI 10.1152/jn.1953.16.1.37. Crossref Y.
  - Claim: center-surround receptive fields in retinal ganglion cells → DoG encoding.
- Enroth-Cugell C, Robson JG (1966). "The contrast sensitivity of retinal ganglion cells of the cat." J Physiol 187:517-552. DOI 10.1113/jphysiol.1966.sp008107. Crossref Y.
  - Claim: ON/OFF center-surround, linear-ish contrast sensitivity → DoG spatial filter.
- Hubel DH, Wiesel TN (1962). "Receptive fields, binocular interaction and functional architecture in the cat's visual cortex." J Physiol 160:106-154. DOI 10.1113/jphysiol.1962.sp006837. Crossref Y.
  - Claim: simple cells, oriented elongated RF from aligned LGN center-surround inputs → orientation selectivity.

## 5. STDP, WTA

- Bi GQ, Poo MM (1998). "Synaptic modifications in cultured hippocampal neurons: dependence on spike timing, synaptic strength, and postsynaptic cell type." J Neurosci 18:10464-10472. DOI 10.1523/JNEUROSCI.18-24-10464.1998. Crossref Y.
  - Claim: pre-before-post LTP, post-before-pre LTD, window ~±20 ms.
- Song S, Miller KD, Abbott LF (2000). "Competitive Hebbian learning through spike-timing-dependent synaptic plasticity." Nat Neurosci 3:919-926. DOI 10.1038/78829. Crossref Y.
  - Claim: STDP → competitive, bimodal weights, input selectivity.
- Morrison A, Diesmann M, Gerstner W (2008). "Phenomenological models of synaptic plasticity based on spike timing." Biol Cybern 98:459-478. DOI 10.1007/s00422-008-0233-1. Crossref Y.
  - Claim: pair-based exp-window STDP model form, Δw = A+ exp(-Δt/τ+) etc.
- Carandini M, Heeger DJ (2012). "Normalization as a canonical neural computation." Nat Rev Neurosci 13:51-62. DOI 10.1038/nrn3136. Crossref Y (crossref lists 2011 online; print 2012).
  - Claim: divisive normalization → soft WTA / gain control.
- WTA via inhibition: Douglas RJ et al. (1995) Science "Recurrent excitation in neocortical circuits" — DOI 10.1126/science.269.5226.981 returned 404 in Crossref → UNVERIFIED, do not cite w/o check.

## 6. Dendrites

- Larkum ME, Zhu JJ, Sakmann B (1999). "A new cellular mechanism for coupling inputs arriving at different cortical layers." Nature 398:338-341. DOI 10.1038/18686. Crossref Y.
  - Claim: L5 BAC firing: apical distal input + basal input → Ca2+ spike → burst.
- Poirazi P, Brannon T, Mel BW (2003) "Arithmetic of subthreshold synaptic summation in a model CA1 pyramidal cell." Neuron 37:977-987. Crossref Y. Claim: two-layer (sigmoidal subunit) neuron model → dendritic subunits as nonlinear units.
- London M, Häusser M (2005). "Dendritic computation." Annu Rev Neurosci 28:503-532. DOI 10.1146/annurev.neuro.28.061604.135703. Crossref Y.
  - Claim: review; dendritic NMDA/Ca spikes enable local nonlinear gating.
- Gidon A, Zolnik TA, Fidzinski P, Bolduan F, Papoutsi A, Poirazi P, Holtkamp M, Vida I, Larkum ME (2020). "Dendritic action potentials and computation in human layer 2/3 cortical neurons." Science 367:83-87. DOI 10.1126/science.aax6239. Crossref Y.
  - Claim: single human L2/3 dendrite computes XOR via dendritic Ca/Na spikes → 2-layer model support.

## 8. Choice analogs (stochastic release / axonal failure)

- Branco T, Staras K (2009). "The probability of neurotransmitter release: variability and feedback control at single synapses." Nat Rev Neurosci 10:373-383. DOI 10.1038/nrn2634. Crossref N (DOI from doi.org link in search; crossref not run). Claim: release p varies widely per terminal (~0.1-0.9 range typical); local feedback sets p dynamically.
- Branco T, Staras K, Darcy KJ, Goda Y (2008). "Local dendritic activity sets release probability at hippocampal synapses." Neuron 59:475-485. PMC6390949. Crossref N. Claim: neighbouring synapses on same branch share p; depolarization lowers p homeostatically.
- Tsodyks MV, Markram H (1997). "The neural code between neocortical pyramidal neurons depends on neurotransmitter release probability." PNAS 94:719-723. DOI 10.1073/pnas.94.2.719. Crossref Y. Claim: release probability p sets short-term facilitation/depression and information code.
- Debanne D (2004). "Information processing in the axon." Nat Rev Neurosci 5:304-316. DOI 10.1038/nrn1397. Crossref Y. Claim: axonal conduction failure at branch points / activity-dependent spike propagation failure → conditional propagation = Choice gating analog.
